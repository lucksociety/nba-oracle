# Regulation endgame correction

The old engine simulated all regulation possessions, then appended up to three intentional-foul exchanges. It stopped once the lead exceeded six points. That created excess mass at margins of seven and eight. The correction replaces possessions during the final three regulation exchanges with free throws followed by the trailing team's response. Overtime still resolves tied regulation scores. The seeded pilot calibrates full-game expected points to the existing rating targets.

The frozen final Nov. 4 Discord card went 2–3. Charlotte −5.5, Toronto–Denver under 229.5 and Miami–Clippers over 234.5 lost. Utah–New Orleans over 232.5 and Oklahoma City −5.5 won. Original forecasts remain available for grading.

## Chronological replay

Replayed both engines with 4,000 simulations per game and identical seeds, using existing archived basketball data. Each date uses only earlier results and prior-season inputs. The absence proxy infers absent regulars from final box scores and is optimistic about pregame information. These historical periods were used in previous research. This comparison establishes no verified historical execution or profit.

This is a correction to distribution shape with mixed broader metrics. Residual excess seven/eight-point mass remains, and higher future win rate or profitability is unproven.

## Verification

A fixed-seed symmetric-team regression fails on the old engine and passes on the correction. It checks excess seven/eight-point mass, symmetry, settled overtime, scoring targets and reproducibility. Existing probability, exact-venue liquidity, availability, input-freshness, data firewall, routing and automated-trigger tests remain required.
